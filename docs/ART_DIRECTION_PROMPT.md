# Team Snapper — Italia Uno brand and art-direction prompt

**Provided by Team Snapper through Zhiang Wang · 8 November 2026.** The team identified the narrative below as its art prompt. It is a human-supplied creative source, separate from the three cited Gemini conversations. The supplied text is retained below; the practical visual prompt in the second section is a new editorial adaptation for reuse.

## Supplied prompt — original English narrative

Picture this. It’s 1pm in London. You’ve got thirty minutes for lunch, and you’re choosing between the same familiar chains.

Around the corner is Italia Uno: a family-run sandwich bar with stories, personality and loyal regulars.

Our challenge was to help someone walking past discover what those regulars already love.

We’re Team Snapper, and we designed a brand and digital experience that introduce the café before your first visit.

Our central idea is simple: “Table for one? Table for everyone.”

Uno means one. We connected that name with the café’s sense of togetherness, helping nearby students and workers feel welcome from their first visit.

Three principles guide the brand: homemade, together and generous. We kept the wording friendly and straightforward, taking our cue from how the family talks to customers.

Our logo combines handwritten lettering and café imagery. The palette draws from the shop’s blue signage, tomato red and paper-bag cream.

We wanted to honour and amplify what already makes Italia Uno special. We brought its tiles, Italian flag bunting and handwritten chalkboard menus into our website and campaign designs, carrying the café’s familiar character into new places.

In the web app, an illustrated host welcomes visitors into a playful digital café, where they can explore, discover the story and play. Simulated purchases remain virtual.

Our Instagram campaign takes people from discovering the café to planning a visit. Food posts show what’s on the menu; location posts show where to find it; and family introductions help people get to know the owners. Game previews direct viewers to the link in our bio. Stories use polls and countdowns to build interest, followed by a direct link to play. Reels show the game alongside the real café, inviting viewers to visit in person.

We want the experience to be easy for as many people as possible to use, with readable text, clear navigation and support for different screen sizes. These accessibility goals need testing.

Our vision extends across computers, tablets and an interactive ordering screen inside the café.

The wider system would support automated Instagram publishing through prepared, scheduled posts. The proposed ordering screen would let customers browse and order; real payments and connection to the café’s order system would require further implementation.

When we asked Gemini to help explain our process, its first response mostly described the finished brand. We pushed back: explain the choices behind it. That exchange is in our log, including our feedback and its revision.

For the next stage, we’d give a Custom Gem the café’s story and brand guidelines, so we wouldn’t have to explain them again for every new task. We’d explore visual ideas in Nano Banana using shop photographs as references, then build editable Canva templates so the café team could update a special or price themselves.

Other independent businesses could adapt this approach around their own stories, identities and menus.

Italia Uno is our starting point, with its personality guiding the experience.

Your first visit should feel like your usual.

## Reusable visual prompt — derived adaptation

**This is a new prompt distilled from the supplied narrative and the team's recorded visual feedback. It is not a verbatim historical Gemini prompt.**

```text
Develop the visual direction for Italia Uno, a welcoming independent café
experience by Team Snapper. The central idea is “Table for one? Table for
everyone.” The three brand principles are homemade, together and generous.
The feeling should be: “Your first visit should feel like your usual.”

Use the supplied Italia Uno logo and restaurant photographs as the visual
anchors. Create a coherent handcrafted café illustration language: organic
indigo/blue linework, warm paper-bag cream, subtle paper texture, and controlled
tomato-red accents. Keep the warmth and individuality of the real shop.

Carry recognisable details into the shopfront and interior: its blue signage,
ceramic tiles, Italian flag bunting, handwritten chalkboard menus, food counter,
coffee equipment, small tables and chairs. Keep the spaces richly observed
and proportionate, with a clear focal point and a friendly illustrated host.

Match display typography to the supplied logo: substantial, rounded, broad
handwritten brush forms. Pair it with clear reading text for menus, dialogue,
receipts and navigation. Repeat the same type, linework, colour and icon rules
across the game, keepsake receipt and campaign layouts.

On desktop, let the café scene lead the composition and place dialogue where
it preserves the host and important scene details. On phones, show fewer
simultaneous information blocks, readable smaller prompts and comfortable
touch targets. Give controls and dialogue dedicated space.

For an art-only revision, preserve the existing game states, complete menu,
interaction pages, virtual ordering, optional story, owner game, music and
saved progress. Present sketches and explain how each visual choice expresses
the brand. Keep proposed campaign formats and future ordering screens labelled
as proposals until implemented and reviewed.
```
